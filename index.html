TL;DR
Distributed Model-Based Diffusion (DMBD) distributes the diffusion denoising process across robots through local conditional diffusion denoising processes, enabling scalable and coordinated trajectory optimization for multi-robot teams. Each robot performs diffusion denoising process on its own trajectory while conditioning on the current trajectory estimates of the other robots at every denoising step. This effectively decomposes the joint trajectory optimization problem into a set of coupled local optimization problems, improving sample efficiency and scalability with increasing team size.

Abstract

Trajectory optimization for multi-robot systems remains challenging due to high-dimensional coupled trajectory spaces and non-convex interaction constraints. We propose Distributed Model-Based Diffusion (DMBD), a distributed server-robot framework that decomposes the reverse diffusion process into local conditional diffusion denoising processes for each robot to perform independently. By decomposing the global inference problem from the joint multi-robot trajectory space into individual robot trajectory spaces, DMBD reduces the complexity of the optimization problem and improves scalability with increasing team size. Furthermore, DMBD provides a modular formulation in which each robot only requires knowledge of its own dynamics, objectives, and constraints, while coordination is achieved through trajectory information broadcast by a server. Extensive experiments demonstrate that DMBD enables scalable, sample-efficient, and coordinated planning across challenging multi-robot tasks.

Method Overview

The proposed DMBD framework extends Model-Based Diffusion (MBD) from centralized trajectory optimization to distributed multi-robot inference. At every denoising iteration:

1. Each robot maintains its own noisy control trajectory.
2. Robots independently perform local conditional denoising using their local dynamics, objectives, and constraints while being conditioned on the latest trajectory estimates of other robots.
3. Updated state trajectories are transmitted to a server.
4. The server aggregates and broadcasts trajectory estimates to the robots for the next denoising iteration.

Advantages

  • 🤖 Scalability: DMBD decomposes inference from the joint multi-robot trajectory space into individual robot trajectory spaces, improving scalability with increasing team size.
  • 🧩 Modularity: Each robot only requires knowledge of its own dynamics, objectives, and constraints, without requiring a global model of other robots.
  • 🚀 Computational Efficiency: DMBD distributes local conditional denoising processes across robots and executes them in parallel, reducing planning time. It achieves sub-second planning for large-scale problems with up to 9,800 optimization dimensions (20 robots, 245 time steps, and 2D control inputs), comared to 5+ seconds for centralized MBD.

Experimental Results

We evaluate DMBD on multiple multi-robot coordination tasks:
  • Goal swapping with circular robots.
  • Goal swapping with rectangular vehicles.
  • Multi-floor heterogeneous robot coverage.
  • Parking and rush-hour coordination scenarios.
DMBD achieves strong scalability by maintaining high success rates while reducing planning time as the number of robots increases.

Comparison

Our method is compared against Cross Entropy Method (CEM) and Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI), Model-Based Diffusion (MBD), and D4orm. DMBD achieves stronger scalability by maintaining high success rates while incurring the least planning time across all tasks and robot team sizes. While the performance of the baseline methods degrades rapidly with increasing team size, DMBD maintains relatively high success rates.
